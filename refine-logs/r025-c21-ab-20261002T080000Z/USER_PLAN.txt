下面这五个设置应该围绕一个单一问题组织，而不是再作为五个新的方法比较：

$$
\boxed{
\text{为什么由第一次 curvature probe 选择的 relocation point 会产生更好的 }g_2?
}
$$

建议第一阶段全部固定在当前已经出现清晰现象的 $r=0.25$ 设置上，不先扩展到 $r=1$。这样可以直接复用现有 $g_0,g_1,g_2,g_3$ 结果作为基准。

```latex
\section{Mechanistic Ablation of the Relocated Gradient $g_2$}

\subsection{Research question}

The completed $r=0.25$ experiments show that the relocated gradient
$g_2$ already achieves essentially the full benefit observed with
$g_3$, whereas the additional inner probe from $g_2$ to $g_3$ provides
no clear improvement after matching the effective update magnitude.
We therefore focus on the earlier transition
$$
g_0
\longrightarrow
g_1-g_0
\longrightarrow
\theta_r
\longrightarrow
g_2,
$$
and ask
$$
\boxed{
\text{What property of the first curvature-derived relocation
makes $g_2$ a better continual-learning update?}
}
$$

The original construction is
$$
g_0
=
\nabla_\phi L(\phi),
$$
$$
u_0
=
\frac{g_0}{\|g_0\|_2},
$$
$$
g_1
=
\nabla_\phi L(\phi+\rho u_0),
$$
$$
c_{10}
:=
g_1-g_0,
$$
$$
v_{\mathrm{FD}}
=
\frac{c_{10}}{\|c_{10}\|_2},
$$
$$
\phi_r
=
\phi+r v_{\mathrm{FD}},
$$
$$
g_2
=
\nabla_\phi L(\phi_r).
$$

We additionally define
$$
c_{21}
:=
g_2-g_1,
$$
and
$$
c_{20}
:=
g_2-g_0
=
c_{10}+c_{21}.
$$

Under a local Taylor approximation,
$$
c_{10}
=
\rho H_\phi u_0
+
O(\rho^2),
$$
whereas
$$
c_{20}
\approx
r H_\phi
\frac{H_\phi u_0}{\|H_\phi u_0\|_2}.
$$
Hence, in the idealized local quadratic regime, $c_{10}$ approximately
contains one application of the curvature operator, while $c_{20}$
contains a stronger spectral filtering effect.

\subsection{Common experimental protocol}

All five experiments use exactly the completed $r=0.25$ protocol:
$$
r=0.25,
\qquad
\rho=0.05,
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}=10,
\qquad
\operatorname{batch}=128,
$$
with seed $2$, $50$ epochs per task, the same task ordering, the same
task and epoch specific data RNG streams, and isolated evaluation RNG.

The optimizer configuration is unchanged:
$$
\eta_1=0.04,
\qquad
\eta_{t>1}=0.02,
$$
with cosine decay to $0.1$ of the initial learning rate, no momentum,
no weight decay, and no Fisher, EWC, weight merging, or orthogonal
correction.

The five new configurations must reuse exactly the same minibatch for
all gradient evaluations involved in constructing one update. This is
necessary to prevent minibatch variation from being mistaken for a
geometric effect.

Two results are recorded for every direction based experiment:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item the native update produced by the corresponding construction;
    \item an effective-step-matched update, where the final effective
    LoRA weight displacement is matched to the designated baseline.
\end{enumerate}

For a LoRA layer
$$
W=W_0+sBA,
$$
the effective update magnitude is measured using
$$
\|\Delta W\|_F,
$$
rather than only the Euclidean norm of the factor update
$(\Delta A,\Delta B)$.

The primary performance metrics remain
$$
\mathrm{AAA},
\qquad
\mathrm{FAA},
$$
with learning accuracy reported as a control to distinguish retention
improvement from impaired current-task acquisition.

\subsection{Experiment A: isolate the relocation-specific correction}

The first experiment asks whether the information newly introduced
between $g_1$ and $g_2$ is useful even when it is detached from $g_1$.

Define
$$
c_{21}
=
g_2-g_1.
$$

Construct
$$
\boxed{
d_A
=
g_0+c_{21}.
}
$$

The direct comparison is
$$
g_0
\quad\text{vs.}\quad
g_0+c_{21}
\quad\text{vs.}\quad
g_1
\quad\text{vs.}\quad
g_2.
$$

This experiment isolates the information contributed specifically by
the curvature-guided relocation:
$$
\boxed{
g_2-g_1.
}
$$

It should be contrasted with
$$
g_0+(g_3-g_2),
$$
which was already tested.

\paragraph{Hypothesis A.}
If
$$
g_0+(g_2-g_1)
$$
substantially improves retention over $g_0$, then the relocation from
the first probe to the outer point creates a useful directional signal
that can be transferred independently of the $g_1$ base direction.

If
$$
g_0+(g_2-g_1)
\approx
g_0,
$$
while $g_2$ remains substantially better, then the benefit cannot be
attributed to the additive correction $g_2-g_1$ alone. The complete
gradient evaluated at the relocated point is then important.

\subsection{Experiment B: test whether gradient rotation is sufficient}

The second experiment separates the directional rotation introduced by
$c_{21}$ from its component that merely rescales $g_1$.

Decompose
$$
c_{21}
=
c_{21}^{\parallel}
+
c_{21}^{\perp},
$$
where
$$
c_{21}^{\parallel}
=
\frac{
g_1^\top c_{21}
}{
\|g_1\|_2^2
}
g_1,
$$
and
$$
c_{21}^{\perp}
=
c_{21}
-
c_{21}^{\parallel}.
$$

Construct
$$
\boxed{
d_B
=
g_1+c_{21}^{\perp}.
}
$$

Since
$$
g_2
=
g_1+c_{21}^{\parallel}+c_{21}^{\perp},
$$
the comparison
$$
g_1
\quad\text{vs.}\quad
g_1+c_{21}^{\perp}
\quad\text{vs.}\quad
g_2
$$
directly tests whether the useful part of $g_2-g_1$ is a rotation
rather than a change in update magnitude.

We additionally record
$$
R_{21}^{\perp}
=
\frac{
\|c_{21}^{\perp}\|_2
}{
\|c_{21}\|_2
},
$$
and
$$
\cos(g_1,g_2)
=
\frac{
g_1^\top g_2
}{
\|g_1\|_2\|g_2\|_2
}.
$$

\paragraph{Hypothesis B.}
If
$$
\operatorname{Perf}
\left(
g_1+c_{21}^{\perp}
\right)
\approx
\operatorname{Perf}(g_2),
$$
then most of the benefit of $g_2$ is explained by a rotation of the
gradient direction.

If removing $c_{21}^{\parallel}$ substantially reduces the gain, then
gradient rescaling and rotation both contribute.

\subsection{Experiment C: random relocation control}

The third experiment tests whether the benefit comes from
curvature-specific relocation or merely from evaluating the gradient
at another nearby point.

Replace
$$
v_{\mathrm{FD}}
=
N(g_1-g_0)
$$
with a random direction
$$
v_{\mathrm{rand}}.
$$

The random direction must lie in the same trainable LoRA parameter
space and should preserve the layerwise scale of the original
relocation direction. A suitable construction is
$$
\widetilde v_{\mathrm{rand}}^{(\ell)}
\sim
\mathcal N(0,I),
$$
followed by blockwise normalization
$$
v_{\mathrm{rand}}^{(\ell)}
=
\frac{
\|v_{\mathrm{FD}}^{(\ell)}\|_2
}{
\|\widetilde v_{\mathrm{rand}}^{(\ell)}\|_2
}
\widetilde v_{\mathrm{rand}}^{(\ell)}.
$$

Then define
$$
\phi_r^{\mathrm{rand}}
=
\phi+r v_{\mathrm{rand}},
$$
and
$$
\boxed{
g_2^{\mathrm{rand}}
=
\nabla_\phi
L(\phi_r^{\mathrm{rand}}).
}
$$

The outer radius $r$ is identical to the original construction.

Because a single random direction is a noisy control in high
dimensions, checkpoint-level diagnostics should use at least
$$
K=8
$$
random directions. For the full training run, a deterministic random
direction stream fixed by seed is used, with optional replication using
two additional random-direction seeds if the result is ambiguous.

\paragraph{Hypothesis C.}
If
$$
g_2^{\mathrm{rand}}
\approx
g_2,
$$
then curvature information is not necessary and the main mechanism is
generic neighborhood relocation followed by gradient reevaluation.

If
$$
g_2^{\mathrm{rand}}
\approx
g_0
\quad\text{or}\quad
g_1
$$
and is clearly worse than $g_2$, then the direction encoded by
$g_1-g_0$ contains task-relevant geometric information.

\subsection{Experiment D: replace finite differences by an exact HVP}

The fourth experiment directly tests whether the original finite
difference
$$
g_1-g_0
$$
works because it approximates the Hessian-vector product.

Using automatic differentiation, compute
$$
\boxed{
h_{\mathrm{HVP}}
=
H_\phi(\phi)u_0,
}
$$
where
$$
H_\phi(\phi)
=
\nabla_\phi^2L(\phi).
$$

For the first diagnostic, the HVP must be computed in exactly the same
LoRA factor coordinates as the original finite difference. This avoids
changing both the estimator and the geometry simultaneously.

Define
$$
v_{\mathrm{HVP}}
=
\frac{
h_{\mathrm{HVP}}
}{
\|h_{\mathrm{HVP}}\|_2
},
$$
$$
\phi_r^{\mathrm{HVP}}
=
\phi+r v_{\mathrm{HVP}},
$$
and
$$
\boxed{
g_2^{\mathrm{HVP}}
=
\nabla_\phi
L(\phi_r^{\mathrm{HVP}}).
}
$$

At fixed checkpoints, measure
$$
C_{\mathrm{FD,HVP}}
=
\cos
\left(
g_1-g_0,
\rho h_{\mathrm{HVP}}
\right),
$$
and the relative approximation error
$$
E_{\mathrm{FD,HVP}}
=
\frac{
\|
(g_1-g_0)-\rho h_{\mathrm{HVP}}
\|_2
}{
\|g_1-g_0\|_2
}.
$$

\paragraph{Hypothesis D.}
If
$$
C_{\mathrm{FD,HVP}}\approx1
$$
and
$$
g_2^{\mathrm{HVP}}
\approx
g_2,
$$
both geometrically and in AAA/FAA, then the local Hessian mechanism
receives strong support.

If the HVP direction differs substantially from the finite difference,
and replacing the finite difference by the exact HVP removes the
retention gain, then the original mechanism cannot be explained by a
local Hessian-vector product alone.

\subsection{Experiment E: replace one-sided FD by central differences}

The fifth experiment distinguishes a genuine local Hessian effect from
finite-radius asymmetric higher-order effects.

The original one-sided finite difference is
$$
h_{\mathrm{FD}}
=
\frac{
g(\phi+\rho u_0)-g(\phi)
}{
\rho
}.
$$

Its expansion is
$$
h_{\mathrm{FD}}
=
H_\phi u_0
+
\frac{\rho}{2}
\mathcal T_\phi[u_0,u_0]
+
O(\rho^2),
$$
where $\mathcal T_\phi$ denotes the third derivative tensor of the
loss.

Instead use the central difference
$$
\boxed{
h_{\mathrm{CD}}
=
\frac{
g(\phi+\rho u_0)
-
g(\phi-\rho u_0)
}{
2\rho
}.
}
$$

Its leading approximation error is
$$
h_{\mathrm{CD}}
=
H_\phi u_0
+
O(\rho^2).
$$

Define
$$
v_{\mathrm{CD}}
=
\frac{
h_{\mathrm{CD}}
}{
\|h_{\mathrm{CD}}\|_2
},
$$
$$
\phi_r^{\mathrm{CD}}
=
\phi+r v_{\mathrm{CD}},
$$
and
$$
\boxed{
g_2^{\mathrm{CD}}
=
\nabla_\phi
L(\phi_r^{\mathrm{CD}}).
}
$$

At the same checkpoints record
$$
C_{\mathrm{CD,HVP}}
=
\cos
\left(
h_{\mathrm{CD}},
h_{\mathrm{HVP}}
\right),
$$
and compare it against
$$
C_{\mathrm{FD,HVP}}.
$$

\paragraph{Hypothesis E.}
Three outcomes are particularly informative.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item If
    $$
    h_{\mathrm{FD}}
    \approx
    h_{\mathrm{CD}}
    \approx
    h_{\mathrm{HVP}}
    $$
    and
    $$
    g_2
    \approx
    g_2^{\mathrm{CD}}
    \approx
    g_2^{\mathrm{HVP}},
    $$
    the mechanism is consistent with a local Hessian effect.

    \item If
    $$
    h_{\mathrm{CD}}
    \approx
    h_{\mathrm{HVP}},
    $$
    but the original one-sided finite difference differs and only
    $g_2$ preserves the retention gain, then finite-radius asymmetric
    higher-order geometry is likely important.

    \item If all three relocation directions differ substantially but
    all produce similar retention gains, then the mechanism is better
    described as a broad useful relocation region rather than a precise
    Hessian direction.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Primary comparison matrix}

The five new settings are summarized as follows:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\hline
Setting & Update or relocation & Main question \\
\hline
A &
$g_0+(g_2-g_1)$ &
Is the relocation-specific correction independently useful? \\

B &
$g_1+(g_2-g_1)_{\perp g_1}$ &
Is gradient rotation sufficient for the gain? \\

C &
$g_2^{\mathrm{rand}}$ &
Is any relocation sufficient? \\

D &
$g_2^{\mathrm{HVP}}$ &
Is the original FD effect genuinely Hessian-driven? \\

E &
$g_2^{\mathrm{CD}}$ &
Are higher-order asymmetric finite-radius terms important? \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Existing completed runs
$$
g_0,\qquad g_1,\qquad g_2,\qquad g_3,
\qquad g_0+(g_3-g_2)
$$
serve as reference baselines and do not need to be rerun as long as
the exact same RNG protocol and optimization configuration are reused.

\subsection{Mechanistic diagnostics}

Performance alone is insufficient to identify the mechanism.
At fixed checkpoints, we therefore record the following quantities.

First, the basic directional relations:
$$
\cos(g_0,g_1),
\qquad
\cos(g_1,g_2),
\qquad
\cos(c_{10},c_{21}),
$$
$$
\frac{\|c_{10}\|_2}{\|g_0\|_2},
\qquad
\frac{\|c_{21}\|_2}{\|g_1\|_2},
\qquad
R_{21}^{\perp}.
$$

Second, for each candidate direction $d$, evaluate the actual
one-step counterfactual at a matched effective-weight step:
$$
\Delta L_{\mathrm{new}}(d)
=
L_{\mathrm{new}}(\phi-\eta_d d)
-
L_{\mathrm{new}}(\phi),
$$
and
$$
\Delta L_{\mathrm{old}}(d)
=
L_{\mathrm{old}}(\phi-\eta_d d)
-
L_{\mathrm{old}}(\phi).
$$

Here $\eta_d$ is selected only for the diagnostic so that
$$
\|\Delta W(d)\|_F
$$
is identical across candidate directions.

The old-task data are used only for post-hoc auditing and never enter
the training update.

Third, where computationally feasible, decompose the local historical
response into first- and second-order components:
$$
\Delta L_j
\approx
-\eta_d g_j^\top d
+
\frac{\eta_d^2}{2}
d^\top H_j d.
$$

Report the harmful first-order interference
$$
I_j^+(d)
=
[-g_j^\top d]_+,
$$
and positive-curvature exposure
$$
C_j^+(d)
=
d^\top H_{j,+}d.
$$

For a Fisher-based functional diagnostic, additionally report
$$
Q_j(d)
=
d^\top F_jd.
$$

These quantities distinguish three possible explanations:
$$
\boxed{
\text{gradient conflict reduction}
}
$$
from
$$
\boxed{
\text{curvature avoidance}
}
$$
from
$$
\boxed{
\text{smaller functional disturbance}.
}
$$

\subsection{Decision logic}

The experiments should be interpreted jointly rather than ranked only
by final accuracy.

\paragraph{Case 1: A and B succeed.}
If
$$
g_0+(g_2-g_1)
$$
improves substantially over $g_0$, and
$$
g_1+(g_2-g_1)_{\perp g_1}
\approx
g_2,
$$
then the main effect is a relocation-induced gradient rotation.

\paragraph{Case 2: C also succeeds.}
If
$$
g_2^{\mathrm{rand}}
\approx
g_2,
$$
then the curvature-derived direction is not necessary.
The mechanism should instead be described as neighborhood relocation
followed by gradient reevaluation.

\paragraph{Case 3: C fails and D succeeds.}
If random relocation fails but
$$
g_2^{\mathrm{HVP}}
\approx
g_2,
$$
then the result supports a genuine local curvature-guided relocation
mechanism.

\paragraph{Case 4: D and E agree, while FD also agrees.}
If
$$
g_2
\approx
g_2^{\mathrm{CD}}
\approx
g_2^{\mathrm{HVP}},
$$
then the simplest explanation is
$$
\boxed{
\text{local Hessian-guided relocation}.
}
$$

\paragraph{Case 5: CD and HVP agree but FD is uniquely better.}
If
$$
g_2^{\mathrm{CD}}
\approx
g_2^{\mathrm{HVP}}
<
g_2,
$$
then the one-sided finite difference is exploiting finite-radius
higher-order geometry rather than merely estimating $H_\phi u_0$.

\subsection{Execution order}

The five experiments should not be treated equally in computational
priority.

First run the two low-cost component interventions:
$$
\boxed{
A:\ g_0+(g_2-g_1)
}
$$
and
$$
\boxed{
B:\ g_1+(g_2-g_1)_{\perp g_1}.
}
$$

These directly determine whether the gain from $g_1$ to $g_2$ is
carried by an additive correction and whether that correction acts
primarily through rotation.

Next run
$$
\boxed{
C:\ g_2^{\mathrm{rand}},
}
$$
because this is the strongest null control for the entire
curvature-guided interpretation.

Only after establishing that the relocation direction matters, run
$$
\boxed{
D:\ g_2^{\mathrm{HVP}}
}
$$
and
$$
\boxed{
E:\ g_2^{\mathrm{CD}},
}
$$
which distinguish local Hessian geometry from higher-order
finite-radius geometry.

The first stage should remain at
$$
r=0.25,\qquad \text{seed}=2.
$$

Only after this mechanism is identified should the selected
configurations be repeated across additional seeds and at
$$
r=1.
$$

The $r=1$ experiment should then be interpreted as a nonlocality test,
rather than as another independent method comparison.
```

这五组的逻辑可以进一步压缩成一个决策树：

$$
\boxed{
g_2\text{ 好}
\Rightarrow
\begin{cases}
\text{A: }g_2-g_1\text{ 本身有用吗？}\\
\text{B: 有用部分是否只是方向旋转？}\\
\text{C: 任意 relocation 都可以吗？}\\
\text{D: 必须是 }Hu_0\text{ 吗？}\\
\text{E: 真正需要的是 }Hu_0\text{，还是有限距离的高阶非线性？}
\end{cases}
}
$$

其中 **A+B 是解释 $g_2$ 本身，C 是最重要的反事实控制，D+E 才是在确认“Hessian”这个具体机制**。因此执行顺序建议严格按 $A\rightarrow B\rightarrow C\rightarrow D\rightarrow E$，而不是一开始把 HVP 当作默认正确解释。
